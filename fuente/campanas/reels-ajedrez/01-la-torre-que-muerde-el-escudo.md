# Reel 1 · «La torre que muerde el escudo» (aventura gráfica de museo)

**Resumen:** de noche, en un museo de videojuego de los 90, un cursor hace clic en una vitrina con una torre de
marfil que muerde su escudo. La torre salta de la vitrina, detrás aparece un ejército dorado de estilo vikingo y la
escena termina en el ajedrez vikingo impreso, armado sobre una mesa. Para fans de la historia y la fantasía. 9:16, 10 s.

Sale del informe del historiador: las torres de Lewis que muerden el escudo son un dato verificable y raro, y sirven
de gancho sin decir nada falso sobre el set (que es de estilo vikingo, no una copia de Lewis).

Single continuous shot, no cuts. Locked-off static camera, straight-on, no zoom, no pan, no depth of field, no motion blur. Authentic 16-bit pixel art animated like a 1990s point-and-click adventure game: hard square pixels, limited palette, low frame-rate sprite animation, flat 2D for the whole clip.

Action:
0 to 3 seconds (Image 1 to Image 2): the white arrow cursor glides slowly to the glass case and clicks once; the case lights up gold; the little ivory warrior turns his head toward the viewer, still biting his shield.
3 to 6 seconds (Image 2 to Image 3): the glass door swings open, the ivory warrior hops down to the floor with two small jumps; golden Viking-style chess pieces march in from both sides in a row behind him, with a bouncy step.
6 to 9 seconds (Image 3 to Image 4): the golden pieces and a matching silver army slide into place on a chessboard on a wooden table in the foreground; the ivory warrior climbs back into his glowing case.

No dialogue, no voice, no music with lyrics.

No text, no letters, no numbers, no runes, no subtitles, no captions, no logos anywhere in the video.
