focus, but **other images** (the five chess reels all used the same table with the viking and medieval sets). They also

## (a) Visual formats (mostly opinion; no chess-specific data was found)

Ten ideas mapped to the products. The strongest:
- the travel tube pulled from a pocket, then opening into a board (a transformation with a clear end state);
- "choose your side" with dogs vs cats (a built-in poll);
- an overhead push-in over the Greek board, the size obvious;
- the puzzle board assembling itself, part by part;
- the sculptural king on a palm for scale, with a real magnet "snap".

Rules proposed:
1. One real mechanism per reel, shown working, no fake physics (folding, magnets, clicks: viewers check).
2. Movement in the first 1.5 s, text of 3-5 words (secondary: a vendor guide).
3. Product facts on screen (size, sides, board or no board), so nobody expects a board that isn't included.

Secondary only: oddly satisfying content is a large YouTube category; 3D-print time-lapses do well on Shorts (promotional source).

Rules proposed:
1. One keyword per reel, taken from the reel's own question. The reward is always real: price and date, colour options, a measurement.
2. The board comes after a set is shown, never as the lead, except in its own conversion reel. No weight, felt, finish or magnet-strength claims.
3. Social proof without customer photos: the real pieces and the process (the labour illusion, already in our research). Never invented testimonials.

Secondary support (vendor blogs): gift guides grouped by recipient; pairing a hero item with an add-on; the 7-day private-reply window.

## (d) Comedy formats (opinion; chess humour can travel: a Chess.com joke passed 50 M views, press-reported)

Eight concepts. The agent's top three:
1. Dogs vs cats, the pet-owner choice (an instant read for non-players).
2. The travel tube that replaces a full board (the size mismatch reads in one frame).
3. The Greek board big enough to get lost in.

Rejected or changed:
- "the king doesn't fly away in the wind": a magnet-strength claim;
- "te lo cobro aparte" with "el tablero viene en la tapa": not a stated fact;
- the minimalist "nobody can find the king": it mocks the product.

## (e) Soft ambient music under dialogue (secondary and opinion)

- **Levels:** the bed 15-20 dB under the voice while someone speaks, about 12 dB under it in the gaps. Duck 6-12 dB with a 30-80 ms attack and a 250-700 ms release. -14 LUFS, -1 dBTP is the usual Reels target, so the problem was the music's level and timbre, not the master.
- **Speech band:** 1-4 kHz carries intelligibility; carve the music there, not the voice.
- **Why ours annoyed:** a square-wave lead and bass whose harmonics sit in the speech band, a busy melody and repetition (opinion; BBC complaints are mostly about background music, secondary).
- **Recipe:**
  - detuned triangle-plus-sine pads, attack about 1 s, release at least 1 s;
  - a low-pass around 700-900 Hz;
  - a chord change every 2 bars;
  - a sparse soft bell, no drums;
  - Aeolian or Dorian with a root drone for viking or medieval, warm major for cozy;
  - no steady noise (the cicada lesson).
- **Effects:** soften them 3-6 dB where they overlap speech.

**Done the same day** (`skills/pixel-reel/scripts/chiptune.py`, `"style": "ambient"`):
- What we measured on reel 5 before the change: the music sat **8 dB** under the voices while they spoke and **level with them** in the gaps.
- After the change (`videos/biblioteca/nivel_musica.py`, K-weighted): **22-25 dB** under the voices and **12-16 dB** under them in the gaps, on all six November reels. The sound was rebuilt with `remezclar.py` (pictures byte-identical; -14.2 to -14.5 LUFS; no buzz; voices clear).
