## 1. Instrument comedy that travels

- **Playing badly with confidence.** A TV Tropes entry, "Impractical Musical Instrument Skills", covers nonstandard instrument playing for laughs. [secondary] https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ImpracticalMusicalInstrumentSkills (snippet). A blog reads the Trombone Champ game as the joy of playing imperfectly with confidence. [opinion] https://laurakbuzz.com/2022/09/29/trombone-champ-the-joy-of-confidently-playing-instruments-imperfectly/
- **Terrible first attempt, then a real note.** A low-quality aggregator describes a before-and-after format with many imitations. [secondary] I found no standard name for it, and no reliable primary source. The shape still fits a 20 s reel: honk, then tune.
- **The animal reacts.** A dog howls when its owner practises "Frère Jacques" on a flute. [secondary] https://ca.news.yahoo.com/dog-hilariously-howls-along-owners-120025173.html (snippet). Recorder and sax versions exist too. [secondary] https://www.godtube.com/watch/?v=E0JJB1NU (snippet). Experts read these howls as a response to pitch and sustained tones, not pain. [secondary] https://www.kinship.com/dog-behavior/why-do-dogs-howl-to-music (snippet). Production note: a dog is a third character, so it breaks the two-character limit unless it stays off screen as a sound.
- **The carrot flute.** A comedy skit about making a working flute from a carrot shows that the DIY-instrument bit is an established genre. [secondary] https://www.snapchat.com/topic/comedy-music-performance (snippet)

Joke mechanics that fit our rules: a deadpan wizard who expects a tiny whistle and gets a tune; a grandpa who expects a goose honk and gets a melody. [opinion]

## 2. The ocarina's own story as comic material

- **Donati and Budrio.** Giuseppe Donati is credited with the modern ocarina in Budrio, Italy. The 1853 date is tradition, not documented fact. [secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Donati (snippet). A Lombardy heritage record says "nel 1853 (o 1854)". [data-ish: official cultural record] https://lombardiabeniculturali.it/opere-arte/schede-complete/6c040-03451 (snippet). The Horniman Museum places the invention around 1860. [data: museum object record] https://www.horniman.ac.uk/object/M14-1990 (snippet)
- **Conflicts in the story.** Sources disagree on Donati's trade (brickmaker in most, baker in one) and on his birth year (1836 in some). [secondary] https://worddisk.com/wiki/Giuseppe_Donati (snippet). Good: the uncertainty itself is a joke ("la historia dice 1853, el museo dice 1860").
- **"Little goose."** The name comes from the Bolognese dialect for "little goose". Spellings vary (ucarèina, ucarùina). Some accounts say the shape looked like a goose's head, others a goose egg. [secondary] https://www.flutetunes.com/articles/ocarina/ and https://pureocarinas.com/what-is-ocarina (search results; snippet). A 2019 article argues for a deeper prehistoric etymology. [secondary] https://ceeol.com/search/article-detail?id=825358 (snippet)
- **Before Donati.** Clay ocarinas and whistles are older than the Budrio story. The Harvard Peabody Museum says Central America has made and played them for more than 4,500 years. [data: museum] https://peabody.harvard.edu/OE-ocarinas-americas.md (snippet). The Met dates clay figures in the Andes region of Colombia to about 6,500 years ago. [data: museum] https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/502524 (snippet). Uses are interpretive: birdsong imitation, dream states, healing. [secondary] https://sites.duke.edu/dumic/instruments/woodwinds/central-and-south-america-2/pre-columbian-whistles (snippet)
- **Joke options.** Papá Noel: "¿Un ganso?" El Mago: "Un ganso chiquito." Or the shape: "¿Una batata?" "Una ocarina." [opinion] Keep the history to one line; a longer lecture kills a 3-second joke.

## 3. Real features a joke can prove

True in general (confirm for our model):
- **It plays real notes and tunes.** Subholes make the chromatic notes possible. [secondary] https://instruments-du-monde.com/en/blogs/ocarina/how-many-holes-ocarina (snippet, vendor)
- **12 holes give more notes than 10.** The extra subholes extend the range and allow half notes. [secondary] https://pureocarinas.com/differance-10-hole-12-hole-ocarina (snippet, vendor)
- **Range is about an octave and a half, but it varies by model.** Sources differ: "about 1.5 octaves" [secondary] https://fingeringcharts.pages.dev/pages/ocarina-fingering-chart (snippet); a 12-hole listed as A4 to F6 [secondary] https://equipboard.com/items/12-hole-ocarina (snippet); an Alto C listed as a1 to f3 [secondary] https://www.thomann.de/ie/thomann_12h_ocarina_c3_pink.htm (snippet). Wikipedia gives an octave plus a fourth or minor sixth for 10- and 12-hole types. [secondary] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocarina (snippet). Do not say "two octaves" for this instrument: no source supports it for a 12-hole.
- **Small and pocketable.** [opinion] Owner's photos show hand length. Confirm the size in cm.
- **Does not shatter when dropped.** NOT established. Ceramic breaks when dropped [secondary] (seller pages in results, snippet). PLA is brittle, per a filament vendor. [secondary] https://qidi3d.com/it/blogs/print-lab/best-filament-impact-resistant-door-hardware (snippet). A filament maker's study says impact modifiers raised PLA's notched impact strength about 2 to 5 times. [secondary, vendor case study] https://corporate.dow.com/en-us/news/seek-together/pla-filament-toughness-3d-printing.html. No drop test of a printed ocarina was found. [opinion] Do not use this claim unless the owner drops the actual piece (for example from table height onto tile, filmed) and states the material.

Needs owner confirmation (never assume): key (Alto C or other), range, tuning and whether it is tested before delivery, material (PLA or PETG), size in cm, colours, whether a fingering chart or cord is included.

## 4. Franchise tropes to avoid

Viewers of [the franchise] associate the 12-hole shape with these, so our scripts must not use them:
- A tune that changes the weather or the time of day. Search results on the song effects of [the franchise] describe exactly this. [secondary] (URLs omitted: they carry the title)
- A tune that warps or teleports someone. Same source.
- A tune that summons or opens something.
- A hooded or cloaked hero, and a mystical "lift the item to the camera" reveal pose.
- A specific sound cue that solves a puzzle.

Specific risk to our own idea: a floating ocarina that plays itself reads as a magic song. Keep the magic to sparkles on the holes. Nothing in the world changes: no weather, no light, no time, no door. The ocarina never moves anything or anyone. Avoid the words "canción mágica" and "tocá y pasa algo" in the script. [opinion]

Buyer interest in the 12-hole shape comes partly from its association with [the franchise]. [secondary] Search results show a spike in music-store interest after that game came out, but no sales data for the instrument itself. [opinion] The mention is for context only; the reels never name or imply the franchise.

## 5. Concepts (all [opinion])

General rules for every concept: the ocarina plays in beats with no dialogue on top (its notes share the speech band); no sound effect under a spoken word; product named in words in the first 1.5 s; no smoke; no prop passes through anything; El Mago's magic is gesture plus sparkles. Mouthpiece is never at the lips: use a close-up insert at chest height or a POV shot.

1. **"Primer intento" (El Mago's workshop).** Papá Noel tries the ocarina: a goose honk. El Mago floats it, sparkles on the holes, and it plays a real short tune. Joke: the honk is the bad attempt; the tune is the proof. Feature: real notes, playable. Funnel: reach. CTA: "Comentá NOTAS y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: Papá's honk is a POV insert (no face); the honk sound must sit in a silent beat. Production: medium.
2. **"Batata con agujeros" (living room).** Papá Noel is sure it is a sweet potato. He counts the holes out loud. El Mago points at the two subholes: "Dos agujeros para medio tono." Feature: 12 holes and subholes, half notes. Funnel: consideration. CTA: "Comentá DOCE y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: the hole count must match the real piece; check it on the owner's photos. Production: easy.
3. **"Regalo impaciente" (Christmas Eve).** Papá Noel wants his gift now and can't stop playing once he has the ocarina. El Mago takes it back: "Es para el 24, no para el 23." Honest urgency: the last day to order is 23 December; not a delivery promise. Feature: small, pocketable, plays tunes. Funnel: conversion. CTA: "Comentá YA y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: "can't stop" is a joke about a bad habit; keep the honest date line. Production: easy.
4. **"Ganso chiquito" (the rambla, summer).** Papá Noel, sunburnt in Montevideo, asks "¿Un ganso?" El Mago: "Un ganso chiquito, de Budrio." One tune, then the shore. Feature: the name's story, small. Funnel: reach. CTA: "Comentá GANSO y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: the 1853 and 1860 dates conflict; say "tradición" or skip the date. Production: medium.
5. **"Jingle Bells en enero" (beach).** Papá insists on a Christmas tune in 35 degrees; El Mago floats the ocarina and plays it. Feature: real tunes. Funnel: reach. CTA: "Comentá VERANO y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: a holiday tune in summer is a cliché; the tune is public domain but check any arrangement. Production: easy.
6. **"No sé tocar" (workshop).** El Mago admits he cannot play; the ocarina plays itself and he takes the credit. Feature: real tune. Funnel: consideration. CTA: "Comentá CHARTA y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: implies a learning curve we cannot confirm; no fingering chart is confirmed. Production: medium.
7. **"Pájaro responde" (rambla).** A seagull answers the ocarina's tune. Feature: pitch and tone. Funnel: reach. CTA: "Comentá PAJARO y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: the bird is a third actor; it must be background only. Production: hard (timing with a real bird).
8. **"Perro que aúlla" (living room).** A dog howls at the tune. Feature: tone. Risk: third character; off-screen sound only. Production: hard.
9. **"¿Y si se cae?" (workshop).** Papá drops the ocarina to test it; El Mago catches it. Feature: durability. Risk: NOT established; only a real test by the owner can support it. Do not run this one. Production: medium, and claim risk high.
10. **"Medio tono" (living room).** Papá plays a scale and asks for the half step; El Mago covers two holes and the sparkle lights the subhole. Feature: chromatic notes. Funnel: consideration. CTA: "Comentá SUBAGUJERO y te paso precio y fecha." Risk: half notes depend on the key and tuning; confirm first. Production: medium.

## Top 4 (ranked)

1. **"Primer intento"**: strongest joke, proves real notes in one shot; reach.
2. **"Batata con agujeros"**: proves 12 holes and half notes; easy to shoot; consideration.
3. **"Regalo impaciente"**: ties to the 23 December order date with honest urgency; conversion.
4. **"Ganso chiquito"**: uses the real name story; reach; keep the date claims soft.

- Range figures for the 12-hole conflict between sellers. They depend on key and model; the owner must confirm.

- No popularity or sales data for the ocarina was found, and the buyer-interest link to [the franchise] is anecdotal.
- The concepts are my inferences, not tested with the audience.
